# Chapter 1: A Dunk Through Heaven

The night King James disappeared, he was having an excellent game.

With seconds left on the clock, he stole the ball near half court and charged toward the basket. One defender stepped into his path, considered the matter, and stepped out again. James took flight.

For a moment, everything was familiar: the lights overhead, the rim before him, the crowd rising from its seats. He brought the ball down with both hands.

The dunk shook the backboard. Then it shook something considerably farther away.

A golden crack spread through the air above the basket. The court, the crowd, and the entire arena vanished in a burst of light.

When he opened his eyes, he was seated on a jade throne. A purple robe lay across his shoulders, and a crown rested on his head. Before him, a hall full of strangers bowed.

“Long live King Poon!”

James looked over his shoulder. Behind him was a wall.

“Me?”

“Yes, Your Majesty,” said the official beside him.

James stood. “Where am I? What happened to the arena?”

“You are in your palace. I can arrange for you to visit the arena.”

“And my team?”

The official hesitated. “Whom shall I summon?”

James studied him. The man's concern seemed real. So did the weight of the crown.

“Your name?”

“Minister Shen, Your Majesty.”

“Shen. I need you to tell me exactly where we are.”

Shen led him to the balcony. Below lay a city of green roofs and winding streets, surrounded by rivers and emerald mountains. Boats with square sails crowded the docks. There was no trace of the city James had left moments before.

“The Poon Kingdom,” Shen said. “Everything from the river to the mountain passes is under your rule.”

James gripped the rail. His family had been watching the game. What had they seen when he disappeared?

He looked up, searching for the golden crack. The sky was clear.

Then a cheer rose from the far side of the city.

James straightened.

He knew that sound. He had heard it through locker-room walls and stadium tunnels, swelling as a contest drew close. Beyond a line of cypress trees stood a great circular arena, its entrances crowded with people.

“What's happening over there?” he asked.

“The final of the Spring Tournament, Your Majesty.”

James turned toward him. “Take me there.”

At the arena, spectators rose as he entered the royal box. James raised a hand. The crowd roared. Without thinking, he gave them the easy grin that had greeted a thousand cheering arenas.

Then he looked down at the playing floor.

At its center stood a table.

At the table sat two elderly women.

Between them lay a board covered in small black and white stones.

James waited. Perhaps this was a ceremony before the match. Perhaps the athletes were still warming up.

One of the women reached into a bowl and placed a stone on the board.

Half the arena erupted. The other half groaned.

The second woman studied the move. She took a sip of tea.

Ten thousand people waited.

James leaned toward Shen.

“When does the game start?”

The minister blinked. “This is the game, Your Majesty.”

Below them, the woman set down her cup and reached for a stone. James found himself leaning forward with everyone else. He could not have explained what she was about to do, but the opposing player had stopped smiling.

“It is an honor for the finalists to play before you,” the minister said. “The winner has been looking forward to the customary exhibition.”

James kept his eyes on the board.

“Against who?”

“You, Your Majesty.”
