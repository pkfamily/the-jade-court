The Jade Court

The complete tale · 3 min read

Includes the ending. The chapters expand on this original tale.

In the heart of ancient China, nestled between emerald mountains and sprawling rivers, lay the Poon Kingdom, a realm of prosperity and unparalleled culture. Its ruler, King Poon, was a towering figure both in stature and legend. Tales of his strength and agility were whispered in markets and sung in the royal court. But what made King Poon truly unique was a secret only he knew—he was not from this world.

In another dimension, King Poon was King James, a basketball titan celebrated for his incredible skill, leadership, and championship titles. A freak cosmic event during a dunk so powerful it rattled not just the rim but the very fabric of spacetime had sent him hurtling into this new reality. When he awoke, he was no longer on a hardwood court but seated on a jade throne, the people of the Poon Kingdom bowing before him as their king.

To his surprise, the Poon Kingdom was not a land of warriors or athletes but one entirely devoted to the art of board games. Strategy, intellect, and the careful placement of game pieces were the lifeblood of the kingdom. The citizens revered games of all kinds—Go, Xiangqi, and intricate tile-based games of their own invention. Their tournaments drew competitors from across the land, and their grand arenas were not coliseums but massive boardrooms where games were played on elaborately carved tables.

Though puzzled at first, King Poon quickly saw the parallels between his old life and his new one. The court’s strategy resembled the plays he orchestrated on the basketball floor. Instead of calling for pick-and-rolls, he now devised moves on the Go board to outwit rival kingdoms. His legendary ability to "see the court" translated seamlessly into predicting his opponents’ moves several steps ahead.

Under King Poon's reign, the kingdom’s passion for board games flourished. He championed the creation of new games, blending his knowledge of sports with the kingdom’s love for strategy. One of his most popular inventions was a game called Court’s Gambit, which combined elements of chess with a unique rule allowing players to perform a “slam move” that could shift the balance of the game in an instant.

King Poon also introduced reforms to make board games accessible to all. He opened public board game halls and hosted festivals where anyone, from farmers to nobles, could participate. His belief that “everyone has a role to play on the board” resonated deeply with his people. The Poon Kingdom became a hub of innovation, producing some of the most challenging and ingenious games in history.

But his reign was not without challenges. Neighboring realms sought to undermine the kingdom’s dominance in gamesmanship. In one fateful encounter, the Warlord of Wei challenged King Poon to a high-stakes match of Xiangqi, with the fate of their shared borderlands hanging in the balance. King Poon, ever confident, approached the board with the same calm determination he had when facing the final seconds of a championship game. With a masterful combination of offense and defense, he outmaneuvered the warlord, earning not only victory but the warlord’s respect.

Though he never found a way back to his original dimension, King Poon found fulfillment in ruling a land where games united people. His legend grew, and his name became synonymous with strategy, leadership, and creativity. The Poon Kingdom thrived under his guidance, leaving a legacy not just of power but of play, proving that even in a world of tiles and tokens, the heart of a champion could shine as brightly as ever.
